ISRO Completes Ground Static Test of PSLV's Third Stage

November 13, 2026 · World News Live brief · Source: The Hindu

The test fires the rocket stage while it is held firmly on the ground, checking that it performs exactly as designed before it ever flies.

Photo: Indian Space Research Organisation / Wikimedia Commons (GODL-India).

The Indian Space Research Organisation has successfully carried out a ground static test of the third stage of its Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle (PSLV), officials said.

In a static test, the stage is fired while bolted to a test stand, letting engineers measure its thrust and behaviour without leaving the ground. The PSLV's third stage is a solid-fuelled motor, and the test checks that it burns evenly and delivers the expected thrust. It is a standard step in proving a rocket stage is ready for flight.

The PSLV has been India's workhorse launcher for more than three decades, carrying satellites into polar and other orbits, and its reliability has made it a trusted vehicle for Indian and foreign payloads alike.

The successful test keeps the launch vehicle's development and qualification programme on track, supporting the steady cadence of missions ISRO plans in the years ahead.

Key points

  • ISRO completed a ground static test of the PSLV third stage
  • Static tests fire a stage on a test stand to verify performance
  • PSLV is India's workhorse launcher for polar-orbit missions
  • Test supports ongoing qualification of the vehicle
