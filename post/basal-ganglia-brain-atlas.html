Scientists Map the Brain's Movement Centre Cell by Cell in a 25.5-Million-Cell Atlas

November 14, 2026 · World News Live brief · Source: NJL Design Lab

Scientists have mapped the brain's movement centre cell by cell, profiling 25.5 million individual cells in a project published across 27 papers.

AI-generated illustration for this story.

Scientists have produced the first comprehensive cellular atlas of the basal ganglia, the brain region that turns thoughts into movement and shapes habits, decisions and emotions, according to reports of the work.

The project, run through the US National Institutes of Health's BRAIN Initiative Cell Atlas Network, profiled 25.5 million individual cells — 21 million from humans, 3 million from macaques and 1.5 million from marmosets — and was published in 27 papers across Cell and its sister journals on 12 November.

The atlas is a reference map of the brain's cell types rather than a treatment, but researchers say it gives neuroscientists a far more precise foundation for studying conditions such as Parkinson's disease, in which movement control breaks down.

A related experiment reported alongside the atlas showed a new way of delivering treatment in mice, though researchers stressed that human applications remain a long way off, with the full-brain atlas targeted for 2028.

Key points

  • 25.5 million cells profiled across humans, macaques and marmosets
  • 27 papers published in Cell journals on 12 November
  • First complete cellular map of the basal ganglia
  • Atlas is a research reference, not a treatment — full-brain version targeted for 2028
