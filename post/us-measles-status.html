US on the Brink of Losing Measles Elimination Status, Health Officials Say

November 13, 2026 · World News Live brief · Source: Citizens Daily Brief

The United States is likely to lose its measles elimination status as a regional World Health Organization body meets, though the CDC says its response to the disease is unchanged.

AI-generated illustration for this story.

The United States is likely to lose its measles elimination status, according to reports, as a regional body of the World Health Organization meets to review the situation. The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention has said its response to measles remains unchanged.

Elimination status is granted when a country goes 12 months without continuous local transmission of the virus. Losing it does not mean measles is out of control, but it is a formal acknowledgement that the virus has been spreading within the country for a sustained period.

The United States was declared to have eliminated measles in 2000, after decades of widespread vaccination. Since then, the status has been maintained through high immunisation coverage, even as imported cases arrived from abroad.

Measles is one of the most contagious diseases known, spreading through the air when an infected person coughs or sneezes. Health officials around the world regard high vaccination coverage as the only reliable way to keep it from returning.

Key points

  • The US is likely to lose measles elimination status as a regional WHO body meets.
  • The CDC says its response to measles is unchanged.
  • Elimination requires 12 months without continuous local transmission.
  • The US has held elimination status since 2000, built on widespread vaccination.
